# Video material: the prototype section (about 2 minutes)

This covers the prototype section of the application video: Uno Batch, a tool for First Batch's team to match a brand's brief with partners. Everything below was run in the app on 21 Nov 2026, with a fresh store, the six example briefs loaded and no AI key set, so problem tags came from the keyword rules. The scores quoted are the ones the app showed in that run. **All partner data is made up**, and the app says so in its top bar.

**Why the briefs are split this way:** A shows a whole path that works, so the reasons are the story. B shows a brief that comes in fresh. It has the tag check (more on that below), real gaps, and a step that can be logged. It also links straight to Recruiting.

### A suggested change to your order

You asked for B to go gap, then the gaps view, then logging an outcome. I'd log the outcome before leaving the brief, then end on Recruiting:

- Logging happens inside the brief's panel. Going to Recruiting first means navigating back to the board and reopening the brief, which costs about 8 seconds of clicking.
- Ending on "who to recruit next" is a stronger last image than a score ticking up.

The gaps page is now called **Recruiting**, not "Network gaps".

### The feature that shows product thinking: the tag check

The "Here's what we understood" screen is the best product-thinking moment in the app.

- AI turns free text into tags from a fixed list.
- It never sees partners and never ranks them.
- The team can tap to fix the tags before anything is matched.

That's why Brief B is entered live instead of opened from the board.

## 3. Talking pointers (about 290 spoken words)

Each shot has a word budget, the one idea to land, an anchor word, and what to avoid. The lines are prompts, not a script.

**1. Board (about 30 words)**
- **Idea:** today, matching a brand with the right technologist, lab and factory lives in people's heads and inboxes, and it takes weeks. This is the team's board for every brief.
- **Anchor:** *weeks*
- **Avoid:** quoting their website, guessing their numbers, or saying "AI-powered".

**2. Brief A path (about 30 words)**
- **Idea:** one brief becomes the ordered list of partners the project needs, with the best match for each step.
- **Anchor:** *in order*
- **Avoid:** reading out all six names.

**3. Why 87 (about 45 words)**
- **Idea:** every score breaks into reasons that add up. This lab has done a high-fibre bar before, can do shelf-life and texture work, is in Bangalore and is NABL-accredited. So the team can trust it, or see exactly what to correct.
- **Anchor:** *adds up*
- **Avoid:** calling the weights right. They're guesses, and the README says so.

**4. The tag check (about 45 words)**
- **Idea:** the brand's own words become tags from a fixed list, and the team fixes them before matching. AI only reads the text. It never picks or ranks partners.
- **Anchor:** *fixed list*
- **Avoid:** claiming Claude read it if the screen says "Matched by keywords".

**5. The gap (about 35 words)**
- **Idea:** it's honest about what's missing. No manufacturer in the network takes a 300 kg chilled dairy run, because the smallest minimum is 1,000 kg.
- **Anchor:** *honest*
- **Avoid:** making it sound like a bug.

**6. Logging an outcome (about 40 words)**
- **Idea:** when an intro works out, the team logs it. The lab goes from 82 to 89, the card moves along, and the next chilled dairy brief sees the same lift.
- **Anchor:** *learns*
- **Avoid:** "machine learning". It's a plain rule: track record goes up.

**7. Recruiting (about 35 words)**
- **Idea:** gaps from every brief roll up into one list of who to sign next, with the briefs waiting on each.
- **Anchor:** *who to sign next*
- **Avoid:** saying First Batch lacks these partners. This is made-up data.

**8. Honest framing (about 30 words)**
- **Idea:** all of this data is made up. My first real step would be sitting in on live projects to learn how the team matches today, then shaping this around that.
- **Anchor:** *sit in*
- **Avoid:** apologising, or overpromising timelines.

## 6. Recording checklist

**App state**
- Record on https://unobatch.vercel.app, not localhost.
  - Live data is in Neon, so the "No database connected" line won't appear.
  - Earlier test clicks are stored too, so reset first.
- In Neon, clear these three tables: `delete from outcomes; delete from briefs; delete from added_partners;`
  - Outcomes change scores, so skipping this can make the numbers differ from the ones here.
- Open the board and click **Add example briefs** once. You should see six cards in **New**.
- Do one dry run, then reset again, so the recorded take starts clean.

**Window**
- Browser window at 1440×900, zoom 100%. All the checks above used that size.
- Use a fresh profile or a guest window: no bookmarks bar, no extensions, dev tools closed.
- Turn off notifications. If your recorder shows the full screen, hide the address bar or crop it.

**Things that could look off on camera**
- **The tag check note:** if the live site has no AI key, the tag screen says "Matched by keywords (no AI key set)". That's honest, but say "keyword rules" rather than "AI" for that take. If the key is set, it says "Read by an AI model…" and the tags may come out slightly differently.
- **Two curd cards:** Brief B adds a second curd card next to the seeded **High-protein curd cups**. That's fine, and Recruiting then shows two briefs waiting on the same partner. If you'd rather avoid it, use the trim in section 5.
- **The bottom of the panel:** the contract manufacturer step sits below the fold in Brief B's panel at 900px height. Scroll slowly.
- **"Why" on a technologist:** avoid opening "Why" on a technologist. It shows "Certifications don't apply +5/10", which reads as odd on camera. Brief A's lab breakdown is clean.
- **Leftover "you" copy:** "This means you'll likely need" on the tag screen still talks to the brand. It's small. Change it to "This brief will likely need" if you want, and I can do that before you record.

## 7. Three questions the founder might ask

**"How would this work with our real network?"**
Each partner record has the fields the rules use: category, storage, batch range, specific skills, certifications, how busy they are, and past outcomes. I'd load your partner list into that shape and check it against a handful of past projects where you already know who was right. Then I'd tune the weights until it agrees with your team, and flag where it doesn't. The weights today are my guesses.

**"What would you build first?"**
I'd start with capture, not the ranking. That means a structured brief intake and a habit of logging every intro and how it went. Without that record, no ranking can learn. Sitting in on live projects comes first, so the fields match how your team actually decides.

**"What can't this do?"**
- It doesn't know relationships, pricing, quality of work, or who's reliable beyond what's been logged.
- How busy a partner is gets typed in by hand.
- It can say a gap exists, but not find the partner to fill it. That's still people work.
- The AI only turns the brand's note into tags. Everything else is plain rules you can read.
